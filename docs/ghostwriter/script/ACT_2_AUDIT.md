## 3. Where I departed from the bible (and why)

1. **The confession in the Turnover.** The bible had Theo scream "I froze." He doesn't know that yet; Nell tells him later. He now confesses the one thing he does know: *"I said 'in a minute.' I didn't even look up."*
2. **The retention cost.** A vanishing book spine was replaced by something that hurts: Theo forgets his own second novel and the street where he wrote it. June supplies the name.
3. **The midpoint false completion.** The reflection gets Theo to write *and she lived,* not the clause that erases Tomas. That clause stays for Act Three.
4. **Walter's rules are dramatized, not explained.** His faded arm (*I DID NOT KNOW*), a senator who "stopped being the man I'd met," and a mother he knows only from a photograph carry the exposition. In the development pass the rules were made explicit in three short sentences (p. 37): *copying is agreeing with your hands, reading aloud is agreeing with your lungs, signing is agreeing for good.* He also says what the thing is (p. 36: "It writes what you'd like to be true, in your own hand. The more you believe it, the more of it shows") and why truth hurts Theodore (p. 39: "He's built from one story, and a true thing is a hole in it").
5. **The name test.** In the car Theo says "Tomas" and Theodore cannot repeat it ("the neighbor's boy"). It gives Theo leverage and plants the mythology's one clean rule (p. 47).
6. **The photograph, not the dress, reveals Nell.** The pale square in Ingrid's shadow box (Act One) pays off.
7. **Cut:** the vanishing spine, "burn the pages," and the "Dr. Haskell" dayroom info are gone.
8. **Order: the Box, then the Dictation, then Mara.** The bible has Mara arrive first and call from her lab later. In the script she drives up because June phoned her after the near-drowning (p. 33), and one scene does the work of two: the arm, the paper, the blank photograph and "Look at the two backs" (p. 34). No call from a lab, no pencil-hardness grid.
9. **The sixth dimension is said aloud once, in a filing-system metaphor** (p. 38): "A line, one dimension. A page, two. A book, three. The time it takes to read it, four. Every way it might have gone, five. The sixth is the back of the page, where everything it didn't say goes." Theo: "That's a metaphor." Walter: "It's a filing system." Theodore echoes it in the Study (Act Three, p. 69).
10. **June has two decisions before Act Three.** She stays and sets the terms (p. 35: "I'm staying... I'll call you at nine. Every night."), and after the Turnover she phones Ellie, the nurse who said she was at the diner, and Theo does not stop her (p. 57). The second one brings Nell to the door (p. 58: "Your daughter called the station. I walked from the highway.").

## 4. Continuity fixes made during this pass

- Act One's last scene showed three pages on the table although page one is hidden in *Slate*. It now shows two.
- Typed-page counts follow the timeline (forty by the night Priya calls; forty when Theo tells Walter).
- The evidence that the writing is being copied over is visual and exact: page one, never copied, has dark, deep pencil on the back; the sheet he copied from is a faint gray (p. 34). The porch (p. 50) completes it: the pencil is gone entirely, and only the groove shows.
- Fair Copy lines are tracked: wrist (*I reached*), forearm (*There was nobody on the ledge*), ribs, collarbone (*and she lived*). *I reached* fades at the porch (p. 51); the forearm line fades in the Turnover.
- The week: Mara arrives Thursday morning, says "I'll be back Sunday," and does.

## 6. Changes made during the Act Three, full-script and development passes

**Continuity patches (all in the source file; none changes what the scenes do):**

- *The sexton's line* (p. 43) reads "Every Friday. Never did figure out who." Act Three has Nell say Marit leaves marigolds every Friday, and the Marker scene is set on a Friday.
- *June's middle name is planted.* The transfer form in the kitchen scene (p. 26) shows, in her neat capitals, JUNE ELISE LINDQVIST-HALE. "Elise" is the cost Theo paid at the Turnover and the thing he cannot say in the Study (Act Three, p. 70).
- *The father's whistle is planted* (p. 22): Theo whistles two falling notes in the happy first scene and says it was his father's call to bring the children in. It is the cost of the truth he tells Mara on the porch, which he cannot name ("I don't know what"); Theodore names it in the Study.
- *The reflection is "REFLECTION" until Theo says its name* (p. 39), then "THEODORE." The cue no longer gives the name away before the dialogue does.
- *Time card on the Priya call* (p. 27): the email goes at 2:07 and she calls at 3:22, so she has time to read forty pages.
- *The library line* (p. 24): "Only one person ever asks for this reel. His mother, Marit Arvidsen" now follows the headline, so "his" has a boy on screen to refer to.
- *Wording:* "She is waiting for him to say yes" is now behavior (p. 22); "unbothered" is now "nod, and go back to their pie" (p. 44); the diner waitress is introduced once, not twice (p. 44); a wrong (CONT'D) after Walter's exit was removed (p. 54); a stray "Silence." before the scratching resumes is gone (p. 54).
