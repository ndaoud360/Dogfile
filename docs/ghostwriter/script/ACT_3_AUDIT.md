**Formal rules for the two silent set pieces**

- *The Replay (pp. 77–80).* Every one of the eight scenes is staged from where the boy stood. His face is never shown (the bible's rule: the first sight of it is the True Memory), so the script never says anything about his expression. The sound is the original scene's sound from the other side of the wall.
- *The True Memory (pp. 81–83).* Written at a nine-year-old's height without camera language: Theo's own hand (graphite on the heel, carried from the page onto his palm and from his palm onto the child's) is the only part of him shown until Tomas looks up at his face. Sound is written as events (sneakers, breath, water).

## 3. Where I departed from the bible (and why)

1. **Beat 27, the testimony.** The bible intercuts "sensory fragments." The script plays it as a plain table scene, so the True Memory arrives as a revelation and not a repeat. Marit's marigolds are every Friday, not "a card each August" (consistent with the patched memorial line in Act Two).
2. **Beat 28.** The bible has Theodore say "I told you to get rid of him." He never did, so the line became a callback to "Did you ask what he wants?" The writing racing up Theo's neck moved into the Study, where it belongs to his temptation.
3. **Beat 34, turning the page.** The back is no longer "nearly blank"; it is blank to the eye and legible in raking light, because Mara said in Act Two that she knew the pencil had been there "because of the groove." Theodore cannot lunge; his hand stops an inch above the paper. The graphite returning as Theo reads is the inverse of the fading Mara measured.
4. **Beat 35, the Replay.** Same eight scenes, with three changes. The diner is at night, because that is when the scene happens in Act Two; Nell passes the boy at arm's length and stops, which gives her and Tomas their only shared moment before the end. The pointing figure now shows what he was pointing at: Theodore in the amber window. The last scene is tonight's, where the boy cannot go under the water.
5. **Beat 36, the True Memory.** Not a single take in the script (a shooting decision); the beats are what matter: the bicycle, the red jacket, "Theo!" twice, the arm stopping two inches short, Tomas's nod, the pencil sinking, Theo walking home dry. The nod is new, and it is the film's one image of forgiveness.
6. **Beat 37, the Sentence.** The true clause is written in the green notebook, finishing the open sentence the child began ("Theodore reached into the black water and took her hand, and"), not on the back of the last page. A monster is a sentence waiting for a verb, so the verb goes where the sentence is. The sentence now includes the cost to himself: "...and she lived, and he did not, and I stood there."
7. **The name on his back.** The bible has Nell read "TOMAS." The script uses the notebook's signature, "T.A. - CHIEF READER," so the marking pays off Act Two and needs a witness to be read.
8. **Beat 38, "Now."** The bible has June wake with "You were quick." She never heard the pencil's note, so she wakes with "Dad?" and he answers "I'm here." The scene also finishes the reach in both directions: Theo takes June's hand, then Tomas's, so Tomas is the one pulled out of the water this time. That is how he ends dry.
9. **Beat 39.** Theodore says "Tomas" (Law 8 lifts when the clause is finished truthfully) and "Thank you," the line he used in the fog and over "and she lived." Tomas does not rise with them. He walks away leaving dry prints, which is the inversion of the wet ones.
10. **The Waiting Rooms release** is new. Law 6 says the Study dissolves and releases its Visitors; this is where Walter comes back.
11. **Beat 41.** The residents who turned toward the glass doors at "wet" in Act One now turn to look at Theo. Ingrid counts three; Nell has never been counted.
12. **Beat 42.** The bible's "Priya's voicemail" and "June gives him a blank notebook" became: June's label on the notebook (THEO - BOOKS (DO), answering her box label in Act One); Theo reading one true sentence to Priya, which pays off her voicemail in Act One ("Read me one sentence out loud"); Walter's voicemail. The last spoken word is "Now."

## 5. Running time

Act Three is 38 pages against a planned 33 minutes. At a page a minute the three acts come to about 99 minutes; the held sequences (the Verso, the Replay, the True Memory, the flood) run longer than a page a minute, so I would plan on roughly 1:55 to 2:05 against the bible's 1:51 until there is an edit. If the picture needs to come down, the cheapest cuts are the Priya call (p. 98), the Waiting Rooms release (p. 90) and the Perry Street exchange (pp. 92–93); none of them carries the plot.

## 6. Questions for you

1. **Tomas's exit.** He walks away dry across the Verso and does not rise with the others. The only reflection at the end is the sky. Keep him walking, or do you want a final glimpse of him in the pond beside Nell?
2. **Theodore's last words.** He says "Tomas" and "Thank you" before he comes apart. It closes Law 8 and the fog-writing, but it is the most deliberate grace note in the act. Keep it, or let him go with "I only wanted to be the one who did it"?
3. **The denouement is about ten pages** (pp. 90–99, five scenes). Is that the right amount of aftermath, or should Alder Hill and the kitchen call be folded together?
