## 3. Where I departed from the bible (and why)

1. **The confession in the Turnover.** The bible had Theo scream "I froze." He doesn't know that yet; Nell tells him later. He now confesses the one thing he does know: *"I said 'in a minute.' I didn't even look up."*
2. **The retention cost.** A vanishing book spine was replaced by something that hurts: Theo forgets his own second novel and the street where he wrote it. June supplies the name.
3. **The midpoint false completion.** The reflection gets Theo to write *and she lived,* not the clause that erases Tomas. That clause stays for Act Three.
4. **Walter's rules are dramatized, not explained.** His faded arm (*I DID NOT KNOW*), a senator who "stopped being the man I'd met," and a mother he knows only from a photograph carry the exposition.
5. **The name test.** In the car Theo says "Tomas" and Theodore cannot repeat it ("the neighbor's boy"). It gives Theo leverage and plants the mythology's one clean rule.
6. **The photograph, not the dress, reveals Nell.** The pale square in Ingrid's shadow box (Act One) pays off.
7. **Cut:** the vanishing spine, "burn the pages," and the "Dr. Haskell" dayroom info are gone.

1. **Length.** Act Two is 40 pages for about 54 minutes. The script runs a little lean (Acts One and Two total 61 pages for 78 minutes). I would leave it and let Act Three breathe.

3. **Theodore's "bookkeeping error" line** calls the wet figure a mistake. Keep it, or soften it?
